# Finding award-winning campaigns with Awardy

Start with the kind of work you want to see. Awardy finds campaigns by industry, brand, competitor, channel or practice, then explains what the teams did and what they reported. It uses award programs and categories to find the work and includes the recognition with each case.

If you need to connect or install Awardy, follow the [Connect Awardy guide](https://github.com/mrcglyn/awardy-skills/tree/main/skills/connect-awardy-mcp#readme) for your assistant.

## Run a search with @Awardy

1. Start a new text chat. In the message box, type **@** and select **Awardy** from the menu. You can also open **+** and select Awardy where that option is available.
2. After the Awardy mention appears in your message, ask what you want to see. For example:

> Find standout product launches in Southeast Asia.

The sent message will begin with **@Awardy**. The skill helps find cases for your topic, check what each team did and cite the sources. The [worked example](#worked-example--innovative-uses-of-ai) shows what an answer can look like.

## Start a search in another assistant

With Awardy connected, start your question with “Use Awardy to…” Follow the [skill installation steps](../README.md) for your assistant if you have not installed the skill yet.

If you have a brief, use this pattern:

> Use Awardy to find campaigns in [industry] or from [brands] that used [channel or method] for [objective]. Prioritise [markets or years]. Explain what each team did, what it reported and what I could learn from it.

Start with the information you have. “Haircare,” “TikTok,” and “AI in production” are all useful starting points. If you already know the award program or category, name it and Awardy will search there directly.

## When Awardy should ask a follow-up

A good follow-up fills a gap that would change the cases you see. Awardy can reuse information you already shared and choose the programs, categories and editions on its own.

| What you ask | What Awardy can do or ask next |
| --- | --- |
| “Find me the best AI campaigns.” | Start searching. Show a few campaigns that used AI in different ways. Offer to focus on creative production, audience experiences or campaign performance after showing examples. |
| “Find the most recent winning campaigns in my industry.” | If the industry is missing, ask “Which industry should I look at?” Then find recent winners in that industry. |
| “Compare my brand with our competitors.” | If the names are missing, ask “Which brand and competitors should I compare?” If your brand is already known, ask just for the competitors. |
| “Show me the winners in The One Show 2026’s Branded AI Campaign category.” | Search that edition and category directly. |
| “Show me ideas for our next launch.” | Ask what you are launching and where, if those details are missing. Ask about the launch objective only if it changes the search. |
| “I need a different approach to social.” | Ask which channel or objective matters most if the brief is unclear. For example, TikTok creators for product trial or Instagram content for awareness. |

Useful details include the brand or industry, competitor names, markets, audience, campaign goal, and the channel or method you are exploring. If you have a hard limit, say so. “Only campaigns that ran in Indonesia” is stricter than “prioritise Indonesian campaigns.” You can also request medals only or a specific year.

For “best,” Awardy looks for cases that fit your question and give enough evidence to learn from. For “most recent,” it looks at newer editions with published winners and uses campaign dates when the case provides them.

## Worked example — innovative uses of AI

**Changing one word · Save the Children Spain.** The team changed just the country name in a prompt for images of everyday childhood. It used the stark differences in the generated images as the idea for a campaign about inequality. The case reports 24.1 million video views.

*The One Show 2026 · Branded AI Campaign · Bronze Pencil.* [Official case](https://www.oneclub.org/awards/theoneshow/-award/63574/changing-one-word/)

**The Shakespeare Bic · BIC Brasil.** The team used AI to recreate Shakespeare’s handwriting, then used a robotic arm holding a Bic Cristal to write a 226-page edition of *Romeo and Juliet*. The book turned an abstract product claim about how far the pen can write into a demonstration.

*The One Show 2026 · AI Craft – Art & Visual Design · Gold Pencil.* [Official winners archive](https://www.oneclub.org/awards/theoneshow/-adtitle/the%20shakespeare%20bic/2026)

**Dream Bake Studio · Betty Crocker.** Visitors sketched a cake, and AI turned the sketch into a design for a personalised cake mix box. The case reports more than 300 custom cakes created.

*The Drum Awards 2025 · AI-Powered Digital Experience · Bronze.* [Official results](https://thedrumawards.com/live/en/page/digital-experience-results#/digital-experience/2025)

You can narrow the next search based on the work you liked.

> Focus on audience experiences that a smaller brand could adapt. Show what the audience did, how AI enabled it, and which results the case reports.

## More prompts you can use

| Use case | Example prompt | What you can learn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Industry inspiration | “Show me standout automotive campaigns in Southeast Asia. Tell me where each campaign ran and why it is relevant.” | How campaigns in the same industry approached different local audiences. |
| Competitor research | “Compare campaigns from KFC Thailand and McDonald’s Philippines. What audience insights and channels did they use?” | How two brands turned local interests into different creative choices. |
| Challenger ideas | “We’re a small challenger brand. Find campaigns that earned attention with a modest budget. Show any documented spend.” | How smaller teams used an idea, an audience or a channel to get noticed. |
| Product launches | “Find family-car launches in Indonesia. How did each campaign bring a local customer insight into the launch?” | How a local insight shaped the story, rollout and reported results. |
| Creators and communities | “Show me campaigns where creators or communities helped drive product trial or sales. Which cases report a commercial outcome?” | How audiences got involved and what evidence the cases give for the outcome. |
| Channel research | “Find TikTok campaigns for consumer brands. Compare the content format and how people were invited to participate.” | Which formats and participation ideas fit your brief. |
| Compare two approaches | “Compare uses of AI in visual production and audience participation. Give me one case of each and an idea I could test for my brand.” | What each approach made possible and how you could adapt it. |
| Find a known case | “Find Singtel’s Singapore Dreamin’ case. How did it reach small businesses?” | The campaign’s audience, idea, channels and reported results. |

## What to expect in the answer

- **Browsing.** Awardy uses the category name and description to find a fit. It groups results by award, edition and category and shows the winner count for each group. Each line gives the campaign, advertiser or brand, and award level. It lists the winners in each category when they fit. For longer lists, Awardy tells you how many it’s showing and offers the rest. Awardy then asks which cases you’d like to explore.
- **The work.** For a question about how a campaign worked, Awardy starts with a [shortlist of editions with written cases](program-coverage.md). For a request to watch case films, it looks for video links. A question about who won can use any relevant program.
- **A closer look.** Pick a case or say yes to see a few of the strongest matches. Awardy gives a short description, a link to the case detail page, and a separate video link when available. The detail page can be on the award organizer’s website. You can also ask for these details upfront.
- **What to refine.** When a search is too broad or coverage is limited, Awardy can suggest another category, a different edition or a more precise brand, market or channel. A locked program is an access issue. An empty category reflects the results available for that search.
- **How to use the evidence.** Campaign metrics are reported by the case source. For geographic claims, look for evidence of the campaign audience or rollout. A global award can recognise work that ran in one country.
